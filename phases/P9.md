# P9 — Glass UI

**Goal:** every chrome element, control and content surface shares one CSS-first glass system, without a second animation loop or WebGL context.

**Owner decision (2026-11-15):** use an in-house single-loop glass, not `liquefy-ui`. `@liquefy-ui/react`'s `LiquidRenderer` owns its own `requestAnimationFrame` and WebGL context and cannot be driven from `lib/signal.ts`; default use breaks C5, §3.1 and C7. `liquid-glass-react` has no loop but is Chromium-only and still a dependency. The decision and the rejected alternatives are in D-202.

## Locked constraints

- **C4:** text stays `var(--text)`. Zone-aware tones mix the fill from the zone's own `--bg-top`; `chrome` keeps the fixed dark `--panel` and assumes light text. No `backdrop-filter` behind body copy.
- **C5:** no new canvas, no WebGL context, no `transform` on `<main>`, no scroll hijack. The only continuous glass renderer is the bounded `FluidGlass` mark on the single loop.
- **C7:** interaction is a compositor transition. `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` removes the transform and the transition. No continuous CSS animation on a surface.
- **No new dependency.** `lib/glass.ts` and `app/globals.css` only.
- **One vocabulary.** `glass()` is the only surface helper. No hand-rolled `rounded-* border ... bg-*` for a panel, bar, card, control, chip or field.
